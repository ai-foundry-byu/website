About the mark

The O in FOUNDRY is made of the two shapes the AI Foundry already uses to describe its people.

The hexagon is the builder. It is the Builder Profile: its six corners are the six skills a builder grows, Discovery, Design, Application Architecture, AI Systems, Agentic Workflow, and Launch and Learn. A hexagon is also the cell of a honeycomb, the shape worker bees build together.

The diamond is the consultant and leader. It is the Consultant Profile: its four corners are Judgment, Ownership, Communication and Collaboration.

The diamond sits inside the hexagon: builders who make working AI products, and the consultants and leaders who deliver them for clients, in one letter.

A diamond reflects light. Its left half is lit and its right half a shade darker, so it sparkles the way a cut stone does, and the flat mark gains a little depth.

The beehive goes back to Brigham Young. In the early years of Salt Lake City, Brigham Young adopted the beehive as a symbol of industry and cooperative work. It was tied to Deseret, a Book of Mormon word for honeybee and the name the Saints under his leadership proposed for their state in 1849. His home, the Beehive House, built in 1854, is topped with a beehive, and in 1875 he founded Brigham Young Academy, which became BYU. The hexagon, the cell every beehive is built from, ties the Foundry to that history and to the man the university is named for.

The mark follows BYU's mission. BYU's mission is "to assist individuals in their quest for perfection and eternal life," and BYU Marriott's is to develop "leaders of faith, intellect, and character." The AI Foundry's purpose follows from both: "to develop disciple-leaders who use AI to enhance human flourishing." The hexagon stands for the skill a builder grows through steady, shared work, the industry of the beehive. The diamond stands for the character and judgment of a leader who serves a client. The light on the diamond stands for what the work is for: using AI to help people flourish, in keeping with BYU's motto, "Enter to learn; go forth to serve."

The letters are Archivo, bold and wide, the Foundry's nameplate face. The O is measured from the font: the hexagon's top and bottom sit on the cap height and baseline, and its walls are close to the thickness of the font's own O. The wordmark always appears with BYU Marriott identification nearby.
